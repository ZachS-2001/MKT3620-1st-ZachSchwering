Background

I’m originally from Fort Dodge, Iowa, where I attended Fort Dodge Senior High. I came to Iowa State University planning to study engineering, and I spent a few years fully committed to that path. Over time, I realized that while I enjoyed the technical problem-solving, my real strength was in connecting with people and communicating ideas clearly — so I made the deliberate choice to switch into Marketing. I’m now a senior, set to graduate in the spring of 2027.

That switch wasn’t a step away from the technical world; it was a way to build on it. I still think like someone with an engineering background — methodical, detail-oriented, and comfortable getting hands-on — but I now put that mindset to work in a field built around understanding people and translating complex ideas into something clear and compelling.

Strengths

My biggest strengths are my technical experience and my communication skills, and the value comes from how well they work together. Three years interning with McClure Engineering put me in jobsite assistant management, hands-on technical work, and on-site surveying, all in the same stretch of time. Physical construction work taught me to work carefully and safely under real conditions. Writing the operations and safety manual for a water treatment plant forced me to stay organized and precise, since other people would rely on that document to do their jobs correctly. Managing a jobsite alone during a concrete pour meant making real-time decisions with no one to hand them off to.

Taken together, that experience has put me in front of a huge range of people — engineers, tradespeople, supervisors, clients — and taught me how to adjust the way I communicate for each of them without losing the substance of what I’m saying. That’s the combination I bring to marketing: someone who can hold a technical conversation and then turn around and explain it simply to someone who has never seen a jobsite.
