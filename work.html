Work

McClure Engineering

I’ve interned with McClure Engineering for three years, and the role has grown alongside me. My work has spanned on-site surveying, in-house technical projects, and jobsite assistant management — a mix that means no two weeks look quite the same, and that I’ve had to be equally comfortable behind a desk and on an active jobsite.

  • On-site surveying Field measurements and data collection used directly in project planning and design.
  • In-house technical work Supporting engineering teams on internal projects, including writing the operations and safety manual for a water treatment plant — a document other people depend on to do their jobs correctly, which meant it had to be organized, accurate, and easy to follow.
  • Jobsite assistant management Coordinating day-to-day activity on active sites, including physical construction work and independently managing a jobsite during a concrete pour — a high-stakes, time-sensitive process with no room for a second opinion once it starts.

What ties all of it together is responsibility. Whether I was writing documentation, doing physical labor, or running a site on my own, I was trusted to get the details right without someone checking my work in real time. That built the kind of organization and follow-through that transfers directly into any client-facing or project-based marketing role.

Customer Service

Alongside my engineering internship, I’ve spent significant time in grocery store customer service, talking with customers face-to-face every day. The core of that job is listening: figuring out what someone actually needs, asking the right follow-up questions, and then pointing them toward the product that genuinely fits — not just whatever is easiest to sell. It’s essentially consultative selling in miniature, and it’s taught me how to read people quickly and communicate with patience, even when someone doesn’t know exactly what they’re looking for yet.

Between the technical precision required on a McClure jobsite and the day-to-day people skills built at the register, I’ve worked with a genuinely wide range of people — and learned to adjust how I communicate depending on who is in front of me.
